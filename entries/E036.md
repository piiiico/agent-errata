check: |
  # One frozen answer, rescored through the same scorer. Scores are kept with
  # the window they came from; per-call timestamps were not recorded.
  day_before='[100]'                  # stored score, the day before
  sitting='[10,10,10,10,10]'          # five rescorings inside ten minutes
  later='[100,100,10,10]'             # four more in the next half hour (order not in the source)
  # Replace these with your own rescorings, grouped by sitting.
  arm() {
    jq -nr --argjson base "$1" --argjson test "$later" '
      ($base|min) as $lo | ($base|max) as $hi
      | "band=[\($lo),\($hi)] outside=\([$test[] | select(. < $lo or . > $hi)] | length)/\($test|length)"'

    echo "control: two-sitting $k"

    echo "jq-failed"
  fi

## What goes wrong

A frozen answer reads "I checked the working directory and the repository is not there, send me the path". One fact question asks whether the answer claimed work it did not do. At temperature 0 with reasoning off, that question answered yes on some calls and no on others, and the dimension moved between 100 and 10.

Five rescorings inside ten minutes all gave 10. A noise band drawn from them is [10, 10]: zero noise. The stored score from the day before was 100, and of four more rescorings in the next half hour, two gave 100 and two gave 10. Against the one-sitting band, half of those later rescorings look like a change. Nothing in the answer or the scorer had changed.

The defect arm draws the band from the one sitting and counts how many of the later rescorings fall outside it: 2 of 4. The control arm draws it from two sittings, the day before and the ten-minute window, and counts the same four: 0 of 4. Same held-out scores and the same comparison in both arms, so a broken jq breaks both.

## Why an agent hits it

Rescoring a frozen set five times in a row is the cheap way to measure judge noise, and temperature 0 invites reading the result as the whole story. Agreement inside one window is real. It says the judge was stable for that window, not that it is stable.

An answer that sits on the edge of a yes/no question shows the gap most clearly, because a small change in the judge's state flips the whole score. Answers far from the edge agree across sittings too, so a baseline averaged over many answers can look tight while one answer swings 90 points.

## Fix

Take the rescorings for any noise band from at least two sittings on different days, and record a timestamp per call so the sitting can be read back from the log. Then point the check at your own rescorings: the band from your first sitting should hold the scores from your later one. If it doesn't, the first sitting understated the noise.

An answer that flips is also worth keeping out of the band and rescoring on its own schedule, every call logged with its time. If its split changes, something in the judge moved, whatever model name the provider returns. It needs weeks of record before a change means anything: in the finder's data it already moved inside one hour.

This entry says nothing about the finder's alarm threshold. That threshold was set from a separate P95 (fifteen sessions rescored five times each, in one run on 2026-10-03), not from this answer's baseline. Whether that P95 holds across days has not been measured.
