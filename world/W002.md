# W002: Did Lean catch any of OpenAI's first math corrections?

On 8 October 2026 at 05:20 UTC OpenAI merged the first update to [openai/math](https://github.com/openai/math) (merge `fd4aeeb2`, change `30148886`; [history.md](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md) dates it 7 October). It withdrew three manuscripts over a sign error, put out new editions of 27 others (14 revised: proof repairs, corrected statements, clearer hypotheses, one obsolete citation; 13 updated only to cite the revised companions) and added formalizations. In the [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50002650) several people asked whether the withdrawn papers had been checked in Lean; one commenter answered that none had been formalized. A [dev.to post](https://dev.to/slabb/700-manuscripts-48-hours-three-withdrawals-the-verifier-won-dhl) the same day called the question unresolved and was titled "The verifier won". This page shows the files behind the answer, and what else the update did to the Lean side.

This page answers from the repository's own files, before the update (`adc7f124`, 6 October 21:58 UTC) and after it. It says nothing about whether any of the mathematics is right.

## So, did the verifier win?

Not in this round, on the repository's own record. The error that took down three papers sat in the part of the collection with no Lean coverage. Where Lean coverage existed, the papers were repaired and the formal statements stayed as they were. The certificate and the paper are two objects, and this update shows them moving separately: a green Comparator run on `taming_implies_compatibility` said nothing about the cone-sum claim that needed fixing.

The record says what changed. It does not say who or what found it, so whether an internal formalization attempt caught the sign error cannot be read from anything public.
